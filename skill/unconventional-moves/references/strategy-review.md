# Strategy review

Use these checks when a draft feels varied but would not help someone decide.
Revise the weak move rather than adding more output fields.

- **Causal difference:** Remove the titles. If two moves still ask the same person to do the same thing for the same reason, combine or replace one. Changing the channel alone is usually distribution of the same move. Changing who bears risk or who benefits can be a different mechanism if that changes behavior.
- **Context fit:** Complete the thought: "Because this brief has this bottleneck and resource, this action could change this outcome." If the explanation fits almost any goal unchanged, it needs work. Name the strongest reason it may fail here.
- **Useful evidence:** Ask what you would choose differently if the signal appeared or did not appear. Sending invitations measures sending, not demand. Voluntary completion of the next meaningful step may test demand, with selection bias and small samples still explicit.
- **Limited claims:** A two-day trial can test whether a process is usable; it rarely establishes durable adoption. If the relevant event never occurs, the result is unknown, not failure or success. Distinguish a baseline, a proposed target, a spending limit, and an observed result.
- **Real reversibility:** State what returns to its earlier state and which effects cannot be undone. A deleted message does not undo its receipt. Prefer private rehearsal or consenting participants when exposure cannot be reversed.
- **One decision:** Select the move with the most useful learning available within this user's constraints. Explain what the strongest alternative offers and why it waits. Name one first step small enough to start, not a bundle of experiments.

For example, a fictional tool with poor adoption may suffer from setup friction,
unclear ownership, or fear of making irreversible changes. Removing a setup step,
giving a consenting user ownership of a bounded pilot, and offering an isolated
reversible rehearsal test different causes. Renaming three onboarding emails
does not. These are candidate hypotheses, not claims about a real product or
automatic recommendations for every adoption brief.
