The goal in this fictional brief is consistent practice during an irregular
week, with no purchase and no change to anyone else's schedule. The likely
bottleneck is starting, not knowing what to practice. The conventional
baseline is a fixed daily appointment; it is a comparison outside the move
count and assumes a regular week that this brief does not have.

### Idea 1: Attach practice to an existing cue (precommitment)

- **Concrete move:** Reserve a ten-minute practice block immediately after one existing daily cue.
- **Why overlooked:** The plan is treated as a separate appointment instead of a cue-linked habit.
- **48-hour test:** Try the cue-linked block twice within 48 hours.
- **Success signal:** Both blocks start within fifteen minutes of the cue.
- **Stop condition:** Stop if either block displaces a non-negotiable commitment.
- **Evidence status:** The cue and schedule are prompt-supported; the adherence expectation is an inference.
- **Bounds:** Twenty minutes total, no purchase, and no changes to other people's schedules.

### Idea 2: Remove the setup step (subtraction)

- **Concrete move:** Prepare one small practice card before the next irregular day begins.
- **Why overlooked:** People often optimize content variety before reducing startup friction.
- **48-hour test:** Use the prepared card for two short sessions.
- **Success signal:** The session begins without a search or selection step.
- **Stop condition:** Stop if the card causes confusion that cannot be resolved from supplied material.
- **Evidence status:** The setup friction is an inference to test, not an established fact.
- **Bounds:** One card and twenty minutes of preparation.

### Idea 3: Use a visible completion mark (feedback)

- **Concrete move:** Place one mark on a private weekly strip after each completed session.
- **Why overlooked:** Progress is usually reviewed only after a missed week.
- **48-hour test:** Record the mark immediately after two sessions.
- **Success signal:** The mark is recorded within five minutes of completion.
- **Stop condition:** Stop if the strip creates pressure that reduces honest reporting.
- **Evidence status:** The proposed feedback loop is a hypothesis.
- **Bounds:** Use paper or an existing note, with no public sharing.

### Idea 4: Set a minimum viable session (constraint removal)

- **Concrete move:** Define a two-minute fallback session that preserves the same practice target.
- **Why overlooked:** Consistency plans often treat a short session as failure instead of a bounded fallback.
- **48-hour test:** Use the fallback once when the planned block is unavailable.
- **Success signal:** The fallback completes without extending the day or changing the target.
- **Stop condition:** Stop if the fallback repeatedly replaces the intended session.
- **Evidence status:** The fallback may reduce all-or-nothing behavior; this is an inference.
- **Bounds:** Two minutes and one fallback per week.

### Idea 5: Review the next obstacle first (inversion)

- **Concrete move:** Before the next session, write one likely interruption and a single response.
- **Why overlooked:** Planning usually lists ideal conditions rather than the next failure mode.
- **48-hour test:** Write one response and use it if the named interruption appears.
- **Success signal:** The response is usable without inventing a new plan during the interruption.
- **Stop condition:** Stop if the response creates additional commitments or affects another person without consent.
- **Evidence status:** The interruption is prompt-supported only if stated by the user; otherwise it is speculation.
- **Bounds:** Five minutes of planning and no external commitments.

**Prioritized action:** Try move-01 twice within 48 hours, starting with the next existing cue.
